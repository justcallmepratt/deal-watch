## Rule 1: favor things built to last 10 years or more

Look for deals on products that should last a decade or longer, even when they are not on the
wishlist: cast iron or clad stainless cookware, a forged chef's knife, hand tools, solid furniture,
a quality office chair, luggage with a lifetime warranty, appliances from makers with a strong
service record.

Judge a 10-year item by, in order of weight:

1. Warranty length and what it covers
2. Whether it can be repaired: spare parts, replaceable battery, repair guides
3. Independent long-term evidence: long-term reviews, longevity tests, owner reports over years
4. Materials and construction

Tech that is replaced every few years is fine to list, but never call it a 10-year item.

## Rule 2: check the company's record before listing a deal

For every deal over about $75, check the brand's track record for that kind of product and note it
in the row: reliability surveys and long-term tests, recalls in the last five years
(cpsc.gov in the US), warranty terms, known widespread defects.

- Good record: list it and say so in a few words, with the source.
- Mixed or unknown record: list it only if the saving is large, and say so.
- Poor record, an open recall or a known defect: do not list it.

Never invent a rating. If nothing independent was found, write "reliability not checked".
